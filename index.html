Inhabits, Norway Scroll Nikon FM2, 35mm

Statement

I photograph nature in all its contrasts and imperfections. On film, for the grain and the way it holds light.

I. Light, Inhabits, Water

Norway

Norway, raw and unpolished: steep mountains, wooden harbours and the sea. Film keeps the rich textures and colours, and the grain and small imperfections give each frame its own character.

II. Takipsilim, Amos, Buhay Isla

The Last Frontier

Palawan is where I grew up. It’s often called the last ecological frontier of the Philippines, and this recent series shows why: islands scattered across clear water, and the soft colours at the end of the day.

III. Acqua e Pietra

Venice

Two rolls of film in Venice. Gondolas, narrow canals, old facades and the Grand Canal full of boats. Even the bell towers lean a little here.

IV. Azure

Santorini

Santorini, mostly from above. White houses stacked along the cliff edge, and far below them the caldera, a blue that hardly looks real. One frame caught a light leak, and I kept it.

(About)

I was born and raised on Palawan, in the Philippines, surrounded by forest, white beaches and clear water. That’s where I learned to see the world with curiosity.

I started taking pictures as a kid with a simple point-and-shoot, mostly of my hometown and its surroundings. As I got older I started experimenting with film, and it stuck.

Since then I’ve taken my camera on the road, from the rolling hills of Tuscany to the mountains of Kyrgyzstan and the coast of Norway. I love film for its light, its contrast and its imperfections, and for how one photo can take you straight back to a place.
